Strongest defensible contribution framing at this stage

A controlled empirical diagnosis of dense cross-lingual retrieval that separates
the incremental value of hypothetical passage generation from query translation,
and separates finding a known source from recovering its language versions.
The evidence spans eight fixed encoders and three curated source-grounded query
collections with matched cohorts, preserved qrels, and explicit language coverage.

The accepted findings support three concrete statements:
1. For the eligible cross-language single-target settings, translating into the
   searched language improves the fixed-encoder average over original queries
   in all three corpora. The comparison within each setting is matched. It does
   not imply that translation improves every query, encoder, or retrieval regime.
2. The implemented HyDE pipeline adds value over same-output-language translation
   in the EUR-Lex average, but loses in chemistry and UN. This is a corpus- and
   pipeline-dependent interaction, not a causal explanation involving legal
   reasoning, model strength, or hypothetical content alone.
3. In mixed UN search, German translation versus the original produces a
   positive version-nDCG mean difference and a negative source-Hit difference.
   Versus English translation, the pooled nDCG difference is uncertain while
   the Hit difference is negative. The latter comparison must not be summarized
   as an established pooled nDCG improvement. The sign pattern across encoders
   is descriptive and helps delimit the finding.

A useful ACL contribution would connect these controlled contrasts to ranking-
level explanations and reproducible evaluation choices. A new method, a new
metric, a universal language recommendation, and downstream answer quality are
not established. Any practical routing or cost conclusions remain conditional
on later accepted analysis; do not anticipate those results in the abstract.

Close prior work and precise overlap

HyDE: Gao et al., ACL 2023.
https://aclanthology.org/2023.acl-long.99/
Generated hypothetical documents embedded for retrieval are established, as are
non-English experiments. Our implementation and its controls should be described
precisely; this is not the first generative dense or multilingual retrieval setup.

Generative Query Expansion with Multilingual LLMs for Cross-Lingual Information
Retrieval: Macmillan-Scott et al., arXiv 2025.
https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.19325
https://arxiv.org/html/2511.19325v1
The work studies multilingual generation, translation/expansion ordering, and
query formats. Its stated BM25 and original-query-only baseline limitations
leave room for our matched same-language dense comparison. That distinction
supports an empirical extension, not priority over multilingual expansion.

When do Generative Query and Document Expansions Fail?: Weller et al., 2024.
https://aclanthology.org/2024.findings-eacl.134/
Expansion failures and dependence on baseline strength are already established.
Our eight fixed models cannot independently identify model scale or training
strength as the cause; those factors covary with architecture and supervision.

LAReQA: Roy et al., EMNLP 2020.
https://aclanthology.org/2020.emnlp-main.477/
Mixed-language equivalent-answer retrieval and language-sensitive alignment are
central to this benchmark. It rewards multiple language-equivalent answers and
also studies a single-correct-target condition. Merely distinguishing equivalent
coverage from a single target is not new.

SHIFT: Jang et al., Findings EMNLP 2026.
https://aclanthology.org/2026.findings-emnlp.109/
https://arxiv.org/html/2606.18801v1
Its Target-Languages Recall explicitly isolates coverage beyond the query
language, and its parallel pools include translated gold versions. Our source-
success versus version-nDCG contrast is a different diagnostic, not the first
argument that ordinary aggregate metrics can conceal language coverage.

When Does Mixing Help?: Zhu et al., ACL 2026, section 4.5.
https://aclanthology.org/2026.acl-long.1455/
The paper already reports language-interpolation settings with higher Recall
and lower nDCG. A broad claim of the first multilingual metric reversal is
untenable. Our possible distinction is fixed-pool translated queries, canonical-
source success, and multiple parallel positives with an explicit decomposition.

Enhancing Multilingual RAG Systems with Debiased Language Preference-Guided
Query Fusion: Park et al., Findings ACL 2026.
https://aclanthology.org/2026.findings-acl.1353/
Corpus evidence distribution and query-document language alignment are already
identified as important confounds. English preference cannot be interpreted as
intrinsic model bias without availability and composition controls.

Better Together: Complementary Query Rewriting Under a Strong RAG Baseline,
Shanian et al., EMNLP Industry 2026.
https://aclanthology.org/2026.emnlp-industry.143/
Complementary rewriting errors, fusion failures, and cost-aware gating already
have precedent. Any later oracle or selector needs held-out evidence and an
explicit cost boundary; hindsight headroom is not a deployable baseline.

Current statistical implementation: what is valid and what is conditional

common.paired_bootstrap resamples one-dimensional finite values with replacement
and returns the mean and percentile 2.5/97.5 quantiles from 5,000 draws, seed 42.
It has no cluster identifiers: caller aggregation determines independence.

R1 averages the five generated-language outputs within each query/model/method
before forming its paired per-model contrasts. R2 target_matched first pairs
original, translation, and HyDE on the identical query-target-model settings;
then averages eligible targets within each question and fixed encoders within
that question. R2 language surfaces and absent-language contrasts also retain
one paired value per question after encoder averaging. Paired_wide asserts
unique condition keys and complete data; add_fixed_model_mean asserts all eight
encoders are present. These operations preserve shared-query dependence rather
than treating model-language rows as independent observations.

I independently inspected selected_queries.parquet: chemistry has 197 query IDs
and 197 canonical sources; EUR-Lex 194 and 194; UN 247 and 247. Thus question
resampling is canonical-source resampling for this selected cohort. The source-
granularity audit also reports one distinct parent per selected EUR-Lex article,
so this sample does not contain multiple selected article queries from the same
parent. This does not establish independence of similar legal templates or
related sources, but no repeated-source pseudoreplication was found here.

The interval population is question/source variation conditional on the fixed
models, existing generated outputs, configured corpus, and curated selection
procedure. It is not variability over encoders, new generator samples, domains,
question generators, translation systems, model training, or random users.
The 638 selected questions were not randomly sampled from natural user demand;
bootstrap intervals do not repair this selection limitation or provide causal
identification. Averaging eight encoders before resampling does not justify an
eight-replication significance test.

The primary current estimator gives equal weight to questions after averaging
their available target settings. This differs from the report's setting-macro
estimator, which equally weights original-language/target-setting cells. Both
are legitimate declared estimands; neither should silently replace the other.
Chemistry's unequal target availability makes the distinction consequential.
Cross-corpus means, if later used, need an explicit corpus weighting rule and
within-corpus source resampling rather than indiscriminate row pooling.

Two helper limitations deserve attention in final reporting:
- Nonfinite input values are silently dropped by paired_bootstrap. Current
  paired_wide assertions and finite-value audit prevent silent arm differences,
  but any new caller must preserve these checks and report the actual sample n.
- A cell with one source receives a zero-width percentile interval. This is a
  degenerate resampling result, not precise evidence about a population. Tiny
  chemistry cells should remain descriptive, with n visible.

Intervals are unadjusted and contrasts were selected adaptively over rounds.
They can characterize the observed contrasts but are not a familywise confirmatory
test. Do not count five language signs, eight encoders, and repeated cutoffs as
independent confirmations. If a small final claim family is examined with a
multiplicity sensitivity, label it retrospective; correction cannot undo every
unrecorded analytic choice or supply an unseen test set.

Unit, cohort, and causal interpretation risks

1. Source success is an objective at the current document-version cutoff.
   All 638 queries have one accepted canonical source, so Hit, SourceRecall,
   and AllSourceSuccess are exactly equal. Pairwise Recall also equals Hit.
   Counting these as multiple supporting metrics is invalid. Mixed Recall and
   nDCG reward two/three chemistry versions or four legal versions. Their
   disagreement with Hit is meaningful empirically, while the underlying
   mathematical distinction is already implied by the metric definitions.

2. Source collapse changes the ranked units and cutoff interpretation.
   Collapsing a saved top-100 version list yields a prefix of a canonical-source
   ranking, not necessarily 100 canonical sources. A top-k collapsed analysis
   can be exact when at least k unique sources are observed, or a relevant
   source's rank is already observed; disclose boundary censoring otherwise.
   Keep the version-cutoff analysis separate. Do not create chemistry positives
   using transitive translation closure or unrestricted source-ID equality.

3. The control switch between R1 and R2 changes pool, output-language policy,
   and aggregation. Its absolute-score difference is not the causal effect of
   matching query language to the target. R2's within-setting original-versus-
   translated contrast is the valid matched effect for the specified pipeline.
   Labels called cross_language refer to the ORIGINAL query language; the
   translated query may already be in the target language.

4. HyDE versus translation changes text and, for four encoders, encoder role.
   Gemma/Nomic change prefixes; Qwen/E5 remove query instructions. A restricted
   same-adapter analysis is informative but cannot identify the isolated causal
   adapter effect, because the model groups themselves differ. No offline
   association can replace an authorized re-encoding ablation if that stronger
   claim is desired.

5. Unequal documents and relevance definitions preclude a clean domain cause.
   The units are chemistry patent abstracts, EUR-Lex articles/annexes, and whole
   UN documents. Max-over-chunks scoring gives different opportunity counts.
   Available versions and pool sizes also differ. Thus a corpus interaction
   cannot alone demonstrate that legal reasoning is the mechanism.

6. Known-source judgments are deliberately nonexhaustive. A different document
   with identical text need not be semantically wrong; source/citation identity
   can still be the intended target. Missing evidence spans do not automatically
   invalidate source association. Conversely, accepted language versions are
   not independently human-confirmed answer-bearing passages. Retain all 638
   as primary; quality restrictions are explicit sensitivity subsets.

7. Identity translations expose numerical and judgment sensitivity. The audit
   attributes 26 changed query-setting rows to near-equal scores crossing exact
   duplicate winning texts with different accepted source identities. This does
   not justify silently correcting saved scores or relabeling duplicates. It
   does motivate identifying whether small headline effects depend on those
   questions and on hard cutoffs near score ties.

8. Selection is highest PROVIDED numeric grade, not objectively best question.
   Candidate counts are not uniformly 30; ties are frequent and prefer named
   generators. Winning-language or generator shares cannot be treated as a
   controlled generator-quality result. Query-only generation includes fixed
   corpus/domain instructions, and does not establish absence of pretraining
   exposure to public source documents. Actual translation faithfulness remains
   a separate question from prompt intent.

Concrete falsification priorities for the final robustness round
These are proposed checks, not results or permission for new inference.

A. Exact objective decomposition and matched success strata.
   After Round 3 is accepted, verify from saved ranks that first-version and
   additional-version discounted contributions sum exactly to nDCG. Check both-
   hit questions separately from each method's own hit subset. A failure of the
   proposed explanation on both-hit cases falsifies a simple "more versions of
   the same recovered sources" account, even if the aggregate reversal remains.

B. Weighting and model influence.
   Re-express the narrow headline contrasts under query weighting and original-
   language/setting weighting, keeping pairings intact; inspect fixed-model
   leave-one-out means and model-specific deltas. If the claimed cross-corpus
   sign changes under a reasonable estimand or depends on one model, narrow the
   claim to that weighting/model instead of declaring robust generalization.

C. Cutoff dependence and ranking-unit boundaries.
   Inspect the same contrast at already saved cutoffs, with the cutoffs treated
   as correlated sensitivity checks. State whether source collapse has enough
   unique entries to identify each reported cutoff. If the reversal appears
   only at k=10 or disappears after source collapse, report that as an operating-
   point/objective property rather than a general retrieval improvement.

D. Identity/quality sensitivity without replacing the main cohort.
   Retain full-cohort results, then separately exclude audited identity-affected
   source questions and examine clear-quality/no-exact-span strata. Removing
   flagged items must use the same source IDs across all arms. If signs hinge
   on ambiguous identities or flagged queries, disclose that dependence. Do not
   infer that dropping difficult questions improves the benchmark itself.

E. Encoder roles, length and truncation as boundaries, not causes.
   Check whether the HyDE sign pattern is present in the four encoders whose
   query/document adapters coincide, and whether truncated inputs dominate
   gains/losses. If absent, retract a content-only account. A surviving pattern
   still does not isolate role, length, architecture or generation semantics.

F. Original-language composition and semantic examples.
   Keep the UN German contrast stratified by original language and display n;
   compare against both English and original. Inspect representative losses,
   gains and unchanged cases without selecting only vivid confirmations. If
   translated questions alter constraints/entities, describe semantic drift;
   no paid grader or new model run is needed or authorized by this review.

G. Honest selection and uncertainty boundaries.
   Freeze the small final claim set only after describing how exploration found
   it. Any learned rule must use source-grouped held-out splits and avoid test
   qrels during choice. Failure to beat a fixed policy out of sample falsifies a
   deployment recommendation while leaving an oracle upper bound descriptive.
   No correction should be portrayed as converting these six adaptive rounds
   into independent preregistered confirmations.

H. Costs and timings if included in the final contribution.
   Keep measured API accounting distinct from configured price estimates,
   unique shared generation expenses, hypothetical cold deployment cost, and
   cached reuse. Unknown compute prices remain unknown. If comparable endpoint
   timing is unavailable, drop the measured-online-latency claim instead of
   adding scheduler wall times or unrelated batch timings.

Overall assessment
The accepted reviews already reject the largest overclaims and use valid paired
cohorts. The current strongest result is an interpretable evaluation/control
diagnostic. Its value depends on the coming ranking-level explanation, clearly
stated estimands, and preserved contradictory evidence. There is presently no
basis for an unconditional German-pivot recommendation, universal HyDE verdict,
new-metric claim, or causal language/domain mechanism.
