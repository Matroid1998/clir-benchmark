Suggested framing
Sources or Language Versions? Controlled Query Generation for Cross-Lingual Retrieval

Abstract starting point (requires author revision)
Generated query representations can change both the language of a retrieval
request and the information it contains. In multilingual collections, evaluation
may also reward retrieving several translations of one source rather than finding
more sources. We analyze 638 selected source-grounded questions across three
corpora and eight dense encoders, comparing original questions, translation and
single-passage HyDE in five separately evaluated output languages. With matched
target-language controls, translation improves all three corpus means, whereas
HyDE's incremental effect is positive in EUR-Lex and negative in chemistry and
UN. Ranking-level analysis exposes an objective trade-off: UN E5 retrieves 71
more relevant language versions with German than English translation but finds
the known source for 22 fewer questions. Exact nDCG decomposition attributes its
gain to additional versions. Source-grouped internal selection reproduces the
trade-off between nDCG and source success across alternative fold assignments.
We provide query-level results, controlled comparisons, audits and generation
accounting, and show why language-matched controls and explicit retrieval units
are necessary for interpreting these experiments. Findings are exploratory and
conditional on curated synthetic queries, fixed encoders and known-source qrels.

Results narrative
1. Define the two experimental distinctions before presenting scores: original
   versus translated query versus added hypothetical content; source discovery
   versus parallel-language-version recovery. Cite the closest expansion,
   multilingual-equivalence and metric-trade-off work.
2. Describe selection and comparability. Disclose 10,691 available candidates,
   638 winners, variable pools and preferred-generator ties; source granularity,
   language availability, one-source qrels, prefix roles and the single-passage
   HyDE configuration. Avoid implying independent real-user queries.
3. Present Figure 1 and the full encoder table: target-matched translation helps,
   then hypothetical generation has a corpus/model-dependent incremental effect.
   Include question-level losses and same-prefix sensitivity.
4. Present Figure 2: exact first/additional-version decomposition, gained/lost
   source counts, both-hit paired subsets and legal source collapse. Explain
   why both metrics can be valid without recommending German generally.
5. Show that objective choice affects training-fold selection. Use Round 5's
   held-out contrasts and Round 6's alternative folds; keep oracle headroom and
   external-generalization limits explicit.
6. Summarize robustness, costs and limitations. Use the strongest claims only;
   move full language surfaces, identity controls, cases, prompt audits and
   accounting to appendices or the artifact package.
