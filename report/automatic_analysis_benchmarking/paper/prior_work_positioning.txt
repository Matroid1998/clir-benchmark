Proposed contribution framing

Position the paper as a controlled empirical diagnosis of generative dense CLIR:
what hypothetical passages add beyond query translation, and how evaluating
language versions versus canonical sources changes the apparent benefit. The
study compares eight fixed encoders on 638 score-selected synthetic questions
from chemistry patents, EUR-Lex and UN documents. Its value is the combination
of matched controls, explicit relevance units, ranking-level explanation and
auditable failure cases. These are useful experimental contributions, but their
combination does not automatically establish novelty.

The central generation contrast is HyDE minus translation in the SAME output
language, holding questions, encoder, searched pool and qrels fixed. This avoids
attributing a translation effect to hypothetical content. Original queries remain
necessary controls: recovering a translation-induced loss is not necessarily an
improvement over the original. The implemented HyDE comparison still changes
input structure and, for four encoders, query/document prefixes; it is a pipeline
comparison rather than an isolated causal intervention on content.

Accepted evidence through Round 4

Target-language-matched translation improves the fixed-model average over
original queries in each corpus. HyDE's increment over that translation is
negative in chemistry and UN but positive in EUR-Lex. The sign pattern persists
among four encoders with identical query/document prefixes, and no input in the
primary target-matched comparison was truncated. These checks reject two simple
explanations without establishing a domain mechanism. Favorable EUR-Lex averages
coexist with losses on 21–35% of questions within each encoder.

The clearest objective diagnostic is UN E5: German versus English translation
loses 22 net source hits among 247 questions while adding 71 accepted language-
version hits. Its +3.81 nDCG points decompose into -2.18 from first relevant
versions and +5.99 from additional versions. The pooled eight-model nDCG gain
over English is uncertain. Thus the paper should explain this model-specific
tradeoff, not recommend German as a superior pivot. Source collapse and matched
both-hit analyses strengthen interpretation; they do not make version nDCG wrong.

Closest prior work and the remaining empirical distinction

1. Macmillan-Scott et al. (2025), Generative Query Expansion with Multilingual
LLMs for Cross-Lingual Information Retrieval.
https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.19325
https://arxiv.org/html/2511.19325v1
Multilingual expansion and translation/expansion ordering are established here.
The stated BM25 and original-query-only baseline limitations motivate our
same-output-language dense controls across encoders, rather than a claim to
introduce multilingual generative expansion.

2. Roy et al. (2020), LAReQA.
https://aclanthology.org/2020.emnlp-main.477/
Mixed-language equivalent-answer retrieval and same-language alignment are
central concerns. Our contribution can document how controlled query choices
change source success versus parallel-version coverage in specialized corpora;
distinguishing single-target success from multilingual equivalents is not new.

3. Jang et al. (2026), SHIFT.
https://aclanthology.org/2026.findings-emnlp.109/
SHIFT explicitly measures relevant-document coverage outside the query language.
Our fixed-qrel source/version decomposition answers a different question. A
query-relative target-only metric would change its positive subset when query
language changes, so our treatment comparisons retain invariant qrels.

4. Zhu et al. (2026), When Does Mixing Help?
https://aclanthology.org/2026.acl-long.1455/
Language interpolation already yields Recall–nDCG disagreements. Our narrower
distinction is canonical-source success versus multiple accepted parallel
versions, explained using their rank contributions. Neither metric reversal
nor choosing query languages is itself an unclaimed research area.

5. Weller et al. (2024), When do Generative Query and Document Expansions Fail?
https://aclanthology.org/2024.findings-eacl.134/
Expansion failures and baseline-dependent gains are established. Our matched
multilingual comparisons and question-level losses add conditional evidence;
eight heterogeneous encoders cannot identify a causal model-strength law.

External validation required for broader CLIR claims

- Evaluate frozen comparisons on an independent, nonparallel multilingual corpus
  with human-authored queries and pooled human relevance judgments, including
  relevant non-source documents. This tests dependence on synthetic selection
  and known-source qrels.
- Replicate on unseen source documents and domains with controlled original-
  language and gold-version availability; add genuinely lower-resource languages.
  Preserve fixed-qrel and source/version reporting.
- Repeat translation/HyDE with another generator and multiple generation samples;
  independently assess meaning preservation on a blinded multilingual sample.
  Re-encode matched inputs under both encoder roles to isolate that difference.
- Freeze headline contrasts and evaluate them on an untouched holdout with
  source-grouped inference and declared weighting/cutoffs. Later internal
  cross-validation cannot substitute for this external confirmation.
