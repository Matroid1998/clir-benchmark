Six-round CLIR analysis: findings for a paper

The strongest contribution is a controlled empirical study of two distinctions:
what hypothetical passage generation adds beyond translation, and whether an
evaluation rewards finding a source or recovering more of its language versions.
The study covers 638 selected source-grounded questions, eight fixed encoders,
three corpora and 264 matched document-level reports. The six rounds, visual
reviews, scripts, complete tables and provenance audits are preserved alongside
this summary. No new retrieval, model inference or paid API calls were made.

1. A translation control changes the conclusion about HyDE.

In cross-language single-target search, translating into the searched language
improves mean nDCG@10 over the original query by 8.29 points in chemistry,
9.84 in EUR-Lex and 6.27 in UN. HyDE in that same language changes nDCG relative
to translation by -3.21, +4.26 and -4.22 points, respectively. These are paired
source-question means across eight fixed encoders, with targets averaged within
each question first. See compact Figure 1 and Round 2 Tables 1-2.

All eight encoders have negative chemistry and positive EUR-Lex point contrasts;
UN has six negative and two positive contrasts. Thus a single grand average
conceals useful corpus/model differences. The corpus mean signs survive quality
and identity-control exclusions, language/setting weighting, and omission of
any single encoder. They also occur among the four encoders whose query and
document prefixes coincide. None of the primary query encodings was truncated.
These checks narrow simple explanations but do not identify a causal domain,
text-length or adapter effect. The implemented HyDE uses one generated passage;
it does not cover every configuration of the original HyDE algorithm.

Community implication: compare hypothetical generation with translation into
the SAME language on the SAME questions and target pool. Report model-specific
effects and question-level losses alongside the mean. Positive EUR-Lex means
still coexist with losses for 21-35% of questions within individual encoders.

2. Better version-level nDCG can mean finding fewer known sources.

In UN mixed search, E5 with German rather than English translation retrieves
71 more accepted language-version hits but finds the source for 22 fewer of
247 questions. Its +3.81 nDCG points decompose exactly into -2.18 from the first
relevant version and +5.99 from additional versions. EmbeddingGemma likewise
adds 32 version hits but loses 20 source hits, while nDCG rises 2.46 points.
On paired questions where both translations find the source, the additional-
version effect remains. See compact Figure 2 and Round 3 Tables 2-4.

The all-eight-encoder German-versus-English nDCG change is uncertain: +0.22
points, with an exploratory 95% interval of -0.71 to +1.14. Its source Hit@10
change is -6.93 points [-9.16,-4.81]. We do not recommend German generally.
At top 100, German still has lower source Hit (-3.49 points) but higher version
Recall (+2.15). Collapsing legal rankings to unique sources also removes the
apparent advantage: source nDCG@10 changes by -3.98 points on average. All legal
top-100 lists contain enough unique sources to observe the collapsed top 10.

Community implication: state whether the task values source discovery, finding
an answer in a requested language, or recovering parallel versions. Report
source success alongside version-level ranking metrics. Both are valid goals;
their preferred systems differ. Here Hit, SourceRecall and AllSourceSuccess
are identical because each query has one required canonical source. They must
not be presented as three independent confirmations. Chemistry uses directly
accepted translations, so unrestricted canonical-source collapse is not applied.

3. The validation objective changes held-out system choices.

Five-fold internal selection chooses one of 11 variants for each corpus/model
using only training questions. On UN, selecting for nDCG rather than source Hit
gives +1.96 held-out nDCG points but -3.04 Hit points in the original split.
Across ten refitted fold assignments, the differences remain +1.41 to +2.26
nDCG and -3.54 to -1.92 Hit points. On EUR-Lex the same trade-off ranges over
+0.10 to +1.42 nDCG and -4.77 to -2.77 Hit points. See Round 5 and Round 6 Table 5.

This is an empirical consequence of objective choice, not a proposed routing
method. Internal folds were designed after exploring this dataset; they are not
an untouched external evaluation. Bootstrap intervals condition on the fitted
choices, and the alternative splits are correlated. The per-query oracle is
reported only as an upper bound. Its large headroom does not show that a useful
query-level router has been learned.

4. Answer plausibility alone is an insufficient retrieval diagnostic.

The rule-selected chemistry failure generates a spatial answer broadly
consistent with the saved answer, yet BGE's first accepted source falls from
rank 1 to 19. A EUR-Lex gain adds the saved answer's network name and moves the
source from rank 17 to 1. A UN example keeps first rank 1 while expanding accepted
version coverage from one to four. These six audited cases illustrate observed
behavior; their extreme selection does not estimate frequency, factuality or
causality. Full texts, source provenance and ranks are in Round 4/cases.

Accounting and reproducibility

Recorded configured-price generation cost is $0.72763552: translation $0.230011
and HyDE $0.49762452. All four failed attempts are retained. These estimates
include observed prompt-cache discounts and are not verified invoices or fully
cold forecasts. Outputs are reused across all encoders and settings. Summing
every saved manifest would double-count older compatible artifacts. Generation
attempt durations, amortized offline embedding and amortized offline retrieval
are reported separately; compute prices and online end-to-end latency remain
unknown. The analysis itself incurs no new generation expense.

Claim boundaries and paper positioning

The potential contribution is the combination of matched dense multilingual
controls, a ranking-level diagnosis of source/version trade-offs, and explicit
selection consequences on three curated corpora. Prior work already studies
HyDE, multilingual expansion, parallel-language retrieval, language alignment,
expansion failures and metric disagreements. We make no first-use, new-metric,
universal language, causal-domain or downstream-answer-quality claim. The bounded
primary-source comparison is in prior_work_positioning.txt and literature/.

The questions are synthetic and selected by highest provided score. All 10,691
published candidates were considered; candidate pools are variable, not always
30 questions per source. Corpus domain and source granularity are confounded
(patent abstracts, legal articles, UN documents), qrels are source associations
and not exhaustive human judgments, and four encoders change query/document
prefixes. Independent human questions and external validation are the highest
priorities before making broad CLIR or deployment claims.
