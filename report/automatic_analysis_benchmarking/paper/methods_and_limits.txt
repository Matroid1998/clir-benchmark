Study scope and interpretation

The primary analysis uses the 264 completed selected-question, document-level
reports: 3 corpora x 8 fixed encoders x 11 query variants. Variants are the
original question, translation into each of English/German/Spanish/French/Chinese,
and one hypothetical passage in each of those languages. The generation model
is gpt-6-luna. Each language is evaluated separately; there is no query-language
fusion. The 638 source-grounded synthetic questions comprise 197 chemistry,
194 EUR-Lex and 247 UN questions. Older all-question, chunk-level, BM25 and hybrid
runs have different experimental units and are excluded from these comparisons.

Question selection takes the highest available numeric score for each canonical
source across generators and languages, with equal preference among tied
Haiku/Luna/Qwen candidates and seeded tie breaking (42). This is a selected,
synthetic question population, not a sample of independent real-user queries.
Candidate pool sizes vary and must be reported from the saved selection audits;
do not describe every source as offering 30 candidates. The candidate-pool
provenance audit records what is established by the available local artifacts.

Retrieval ranks each source-language version as a separate document by its
maximum chunk similarity, aggregating all searched chunks before retaining the
top 100 unique documents. Source units differ: patent abstracts for chemistry,
articles for the selected EUR-Lex questions, and full UN documents. Domain,
source length and source granularity therefore cannot be causally separated.
Relevance is the saved source association, not an independent comprehensive
relevance judgment. Legal language variants share a canonical source; chemistry
uses directly accepted translation relationships. Unjudged near-duplicates are
not automatically made relevant by this analysis.

Each question has one required canonical source. Hit@k, SourceRecall@k and
AllSourceSuccess@k are consequently identical in this study. Document Recall
and nDCG can additionally reward retrieving several accepted language versions
of that source. They measure different objectives from finding any source
version. Pairwise single-target and mixed-language settings must be labeled
separately because their relevance denominators and candidate pools differ.
EUR-Lex has no Chinese target pool; UN has no German target pool. Chemistry
language coverage is uneven, including some very small language-pair cells.

The closest controlled expansion contrast is HyDE minus translation into the
same output language, on identical questions, encoders, target settings and
qrels. Four encoders use different query/document prefixes: original and
translated questions use the query role, while hypothetical passages use the
document role. Thus the treatment bundles passage generation and an adapter
change in those four encoders. The other four use equal empty prefixes. This
study's single-passage replacement is not every configuration of the original
HyDE algorithm, which can average multiple passages and include the query.

All 638 selected questions remain in the primary analysis, including questions
without exact evidence spans; source-level judgments do not require such spans.
Saved quality flags (581 clear, 42 repair, 13 blocking, 2 minor issues) support
sensitivity analyses, not post-hoc replacement of questions or source labels.
There are 26 same-language identity-control metric discrepancies among 22,472
paired query-setting rows. The diagnosed cases have near-identical vectors and
crossed relevant/unjudged winning chunks with identical text at float-scale
score gaps. Raw rankings are retained, with exclusion sensitivity reported
separately; a no-op translation is not interpreted as a semantic improvement.

Analysis uses paired question/source clusters within each corpus. Repeated
encoders, settings and variants for the same question are not independent
samples. Eight encoders and three corpora are fixed study choices, not random
samples of all architectures or domains. Model means average encoders within
each question before question-level resampling. Confidence intervals are
exploratory paired percentile bootstrap intervals, not multiplicity-adjusted
confirmatory tests. Internal policy evaluation trains selection only on other
source-document folds; it does not establish external-domain generalization.

Generation costs are estimates at recorded configured prices, not verified API
invoices. Shared generation artifacts are counted once across encoders and
settings; failed/retried attempts and retained cached work remain in accounting.
Generation attempt duration, amortized offline encoding/retrieval time, scheduler
waiting and measured online end-to-end latency are distinct. The last is not
measured here. Cluster computation has unknown monetary cost, not zero cost.
No new benchmark execution, generation or paid calls occur in this analysis.
